# A Seat at Every Table — MyPlate & Cultural Cuisine Virtual Gallery

**Tingting Du** · NUTR SCI 132: Nutrition Today · Fall 2026 · University of Wisconsin–Madison

**Live gallery → https://tingting-olivia-du.github.io/nutri-sci-132/**

A virtual gallery built as a participation activity for NUTR SCI 132. It is organized as three
exhibit rooms, each taking one cultural cuisine and mapping a traditional meal onto the five
MyPlate food groups:

- **Room I — Korea:** the *bapsang* — bibimbap, banchan, kimchi, tofu, doenjang-jjigae
- **Room II — Nigeria / West Africa:** jollof rice, egusi soup with ugu greens, moin-moin, plantain, fura da nono
- **Room III — Mexico:** corn tortillas, frijoles, pico de gallo, nopales, mango with chile and lime, queso fresco

Each room has photographs tagged by MyPlate color, a chart of how the meal fills each food group,
a "flavor without salt" panel on the cuisine's spices and aromatics, and a "where MyPlate pushes
back" panel offering a culturally respectful modification rather than a substitution. The gallery
closes with a side-by-side comparison and four findings.

## Findings

1. **MyPlate is a ratio, not a recipe.** All three cuisines already fill all five wedges — nothing had to be removed or Americanized, only re-labeled.
2. **The dairy circle is the weak point.** Fortified soy milk, fermented nono, small cheeses, dark greens, sesame, and bone-in fish are the culturally valid routes to calcium.
3. **Mixed dishes get undercounted.** Assessment tools built on separate components systematically underrate one-pot cuisines.
4. **Change the technique, not the dish.** The real risks — frying, oil volume, sodium from bouillon and fermented pastes, sugary drinks — are preparation problems, not cultural ones.
