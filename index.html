MyPlate Around the World

A virtual gallery by Tingting Du
NUTR SCI 132, Fall 2026 - Participation Activity

MyPlate tells you what proportion of your plate each food group should take up. It does not tell you what to cook. For this activity I picked two cuisines I eat often and sorted a typical meal from each one into the five MyPlate groups, to see whether they line up.

They mostly do. Both meals already cover all five groups, or come close. The one group that keeps coming up short is dairy.

MyPlate food groups:
  • Vegetables
  • Fruits
  • Grains
  • Protein Foods
  • Dairy
Half the plate should be fruits and vegetables. At least half the grains should be whole grains.

Room 1: Korea

A Korean meal is usually rice, a soup or stew, and a bunch of small vegetable side dishes called banchan. Because the banchan come with every meal, the vegetable half of the plate kind of happens automatically.

Grains + Vegetables + Protein

Bibimbap. Rice on the bottom, seasoned vegetables and an egg on top.

Vegetables

Banchan. Small vegetable side dishes served with every meal.

Vegetables

Kimchi. Counts as a vegetable, but it is very high in sodium.

Protein + Vegetables

Doenjang jjigae, a soybean paste stew with tofu and vegetables.

Protein Foods

Tofu. A plant protein that shows up in a lot of dishes.

Where it does not match MyPlate: there is almost no dairy in a traditional Korean meal, and the sodium is high because of the kimchi and the fermented pastes. Fruit is usually eaten after the meal instead of with it, so it is there, just not on the plate.

Room 2: Mexico

A Mexican meal is often built on corn tortillas and beans. Corn and beans eaten together give you all the essential amino acids, so the protein group is covered even without meat.

Grains + Protein + Vegetables

Tacos. The tortilla also keeps the portion size small.

Grains

Corn tortillas. Made from whole corn, so they count as a whole grain.

Protein Foods

Frijoles. Beans count as protein and also add fiber.

Vegetables

Pico de gallo. Raw tomato, onion, chili and lime.

Fruits

Mango with chili and lime, sold as street food.

Dairy

Queso fresco. A small amount of cheese, usually sprinkled on top.

Where it does not match MyPlate: a lot of the cooking uses lard or deep frying, and sugary drinks are common with meals. The food groups themselves are fine. It is the cooking method and the drinks that add up.

Side by Side

Korea Mexico
Grains White rice, mostly refined Corn tortillas, whole grain
Vegetables Strong. Banchan at every meal Good. Salsa, nopales, onion, chili
Fruits Usually eaten after the meal Mango, papaya, often as street snacks
Protein Tofu, egg, fish, some meat Beans plus corn, some meat and cheese
Dairy Almost none A little, mostly cheese
Main watch-out Sodium Frying and sugary drinks

What I Learned

  1. Both cuisines already fit MyPlate. I did not have to take anything out or swap anything for an American food, I just had to label what was already there.
  2. Dairy is the group that does not transfer. A lot of adults worldwide cannot digest lactose well, and neither of these cuisines drinks much milk. Telling someone to drink more milk is not going to work. Fortified soy milk, small amounts of cheese, dark leafy greens, sesame and small fish with the bones in are better ways to get calcium.
  3. Mixed dishes are easy to undercount. A photo of bibimbap just looks like a bowl of rice, but the vegetables and protein are cooked into it. If you only look at the picture you would guess it is mostly grains.
  4. The actual health problems here are about how the food is cooked, not what the food is. Frying, oil, sodium and sugary drinks are the issues. Roasting instead of frying fixes the problem without asking anyone to stop eating their own food.
