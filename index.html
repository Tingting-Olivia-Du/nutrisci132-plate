A Seat at
Every Table

MyPlate was never meant to be an American menu — it is a set of proportions. Walk through three rooms and watch the same five food groups reassemble themselves in a Korean bapsang, a Nigerian Sunday plate, and a Mexican comida. The ingredients change. The plate does not.

Curated by Tingting Du  ·  NUTR SCI 132, Fall 2026  ·  Participation Activity: MyPlate Virtual Gallery

The Key

Every photograph in this gallery is tagged with the MyPlate food group it belongs to. Use the colored dots below to read the rooms — and remember that MyPlate asks how much of the plate, not which cuisine.

Vegetables Fruits Grains Protein
DAIRY

Make half your plate fruits and vegetables. Make half your grains whole grains. Vary your protein routine. Move to low-fat or fat-free dairy — or a fortified alternative.

Notice what MyPlate never says: it never names a single dish, spice, or cooking method. That silence is the point. A tortilla and a bowl of rice sit in the same orange wedge. Black beans, egusi seeds, and tofu all sit in the purple one.

Vegetables

Dark green, red & orange, beans/peas, starchy, and "other." Fermented and stewed vegetables count.

Fruits

Whole, cut, frozen, dried, or 100% juice. Tropical fruit counts just as much as an apple.

Grains

Rice, maize, millet, sorghum, wheat, teff. At least half should be whole or minimally refined.

Protein Foods

Seafood, poultry, meat, eggs, beans, peas, lentils, nuts, seeds, and soy products.

Dairy

Milk, yogurt, cheese, and lactose-free or fortified soy versions — important where lactose intolerance is common.

The Exhibits

Three meals, photographed as they are actually served. Tap a food group below to spotlight it across all three rooms and see the same nutritional job being done by completely different ingredients.

Room I 🇰🇷

Korea — the bapsang, a plate already divided

A Korean table is built as bap (rice) + guk (soup) + banchan (many small side dishes). The banchan system is, functionally, MyPlate's "half your plate" rule enforced by tradition: nobody eats rice alone.

Grains
Bap (steamed rice)

Swap white short-grain for japgokbap — rice cooked with barley, black rice, and beans — to move this wedge toward whole grains.

Vegetables
Banchan (side dishes)

Spinach, bellflower root, bean sprouts, seaweed, radish. Five to eight vegetables in a single sitting — blanched or lightly seasoned, rarely fried.

Vegetables
Kimchi

Fermented napa cabbage and radish. Counts fully as a vegetable and adds live cultures — but it is the single biggest sodium source on the table.

Protein
Dubu (tofu) & egg

Soy is the everyday protein; beef or mackerel appear in smaller amounts. MyPlate's "vary your protein routine" is the default here, not an effort.

Protein
Doenjang-jjigae

Fermented soybean paste stew with zucchini, onion, and tofu — a protein and a vegetable in the same pot.

Fruit is dessert here Korean meals close with fresh fruit — Asian pear, persimmon, tangerine — instead of a sweet course. Serving fruit as dessert is a direct MyPlate win.
Room II 🇳🇬

Nigeria & West Africa — rice, stew, and a pot of greens

The West African plate is built around a starch and a soup or stew poured over it. The stew is where the vegetables and protein hide — which means this cuisine can look grain-heavy in a photo while being remarkably well balanced on the fork.

Grains
Jollof rice

Long-grain rice cooked in pureed tomato and pepper. Ofada (unpolished) rice, millet, sorghum, or fonio move this toward whole grains.

Protein
Egusi (melon seeds)

Ground melon seeds thicken soup and carry plant protein, healthy fat, and magnesium. A seed doing the work of a sauce.

Vegetables
Efo riro / ugu greens

Egusi soup is cooked with fistfuls of bitterleaf, ugu (pumpkin leaf), or spinach — a dark-green vegetable serving folded into the protein dish.

Protein
Moin-moin (bean pudding)

Steamed, peeled black-eyed peas with pepper, onion, and sometimes fish or egg. Steamed — not fried — so it is a lean protein and counts toward beans.

Fruits
Plantain (dodo)

Botanically a fruit, nutritionally a starchy staple. Roasted (boli) or air-fried instead of deep-fried, it stays a fruit serving instead of a fat serving.

Dairy
Fura da nono

Hausa millet balls in fermented cow's milk — a traditional dairy + grain drink. Fermentation lowers lactose, which matters in a region with high lactose intolerance.

The stew is the vegetable Judging this plate by the photo undercounts it. The blended tomato-pepper-onion base and the greens in the soup are real vegetable servings — a reminder that MyPlate has to be applied to the recipe, not the silhouette.
Room III 🇲🇽

Mexico — maize, beans, and the oldest complete protein in the Americas

Corn and beans eaten together supply a complete amino acid profile, and nixtamalized corn releases niacin and calcium that raw maize locks away. The traditional Mexican plate solved a nutrition problem several thousand years before anyone drew a food guide.

Grains
Corn tortilla

Nixtamalized whole maize — a whole grain with bonus calcium and bioavailable niacin. Choose corn over flour tortillas to keep the whole-grain half of the wedge.

Protein
Frijoles & carne asada

Black or pinto beans are the everyday protein; grilled meat or fish is the accent. Beans de la olla (simmered, not refried in lard) keep it lean.

Vegetables
Pico de gallo

Raw tomato, onion, chile, cilantro, lime. A condiment that is literally a serving of vegetables — and the vitamin C helps absorb iron from the beans.

Vegetables
Nopales

Grilled cactus paddles — high in soluble fiber, very low calorie, and studied for blood-sugar effects. An indigenous vegetable with no European substitute.

Fruits
Mango con chile y limón

Street fruit cups — mango, jicama, cucumber, watermelon with lime and chili powder. Proof that "eat more fruit" does not have to mean an apple.

Dairy
Queso Oaxaca / fresco

Naturally lower-fat fresh cheeses, crumbled rather than melted in quantity. Used as a garnish, so a little supplies calcium without much saturated fat.

Where MyPlate pushes back Frying, lard, large crema portions, and sugary aguas frescas. Cultural fix: beans de la olla instead of refritos, grill instead of fry, and make agua fresca with fruit and water and roughly half the sugar.
Corn + beans = complete protein Maize is low in lysine; beans are low in methionine. Eaten in the same day they complement each other perfectly — the reason this pairing anchors MyPlate's protein wedge without meat.

What the Gallery Shows

Four findings from mapping three cuisines onto one framework.

FINDING 01

MyPlate is a ratio, not a recipe. Every cuisine here already fills all five wedges. Nothing had to be removed or "Americanized" — only re-labeled. A dietitian who insists on a chicken breast and a side salad is adding a cultural requirement that MyPlate itself never made.

FINDING 02

The dairy circle is the weak point. Roughly two-thirds of the world's adults have some lactose malabsorption, and Korean and West African traditional diets barely use fluid milk. Counseling that leads with "drink more milk" fails here; fortified soy milk, fermented nono, small cheeses, greens, sesame, and bone-in fish are the culturally valid routes to calcium.

FINDING 03

Mixed dishes get undercounted. A photo of jollof rice or bibimbap looks like "a bowl of rice." The vegetables and protein are cooked into the dish. Any assessment tool built on separate-components thinking will systematically underestimate the diet quality of cuisines that cook in one pot.

FINDING 04

Change the technique, not the dish. The real risks — frying, palm oil and lard volume, sodium from bouillon and jang, sugary drinks — are all preparation problems. Roasting plantain instead of frying it, or simmering beans instead of refrying them, protects the culture and moves the nutrition. Telling someone to stop eating jollof rice does neither.
